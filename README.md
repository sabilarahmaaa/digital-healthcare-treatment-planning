# Digital Healthcare Treatment Planning & Tracking

![Project cover](assets/digital-healthcare-treatment-planning-cover.png)

A product management case study exploring how clinicians could plan treatments, document decisions, and monitor patient progress across multiple visits.

The proposed experience connects clinical context, treatment planning, and longitudinal progress tracking while keeping clinicians in control of every clinical decision.

> **Project context:** This individual case study was developed from a provided product design brief using fictional clinical scenarios and sample data. It does not represent a production system, validated clinical workflow, or clinical recommendation.

---

## Project Overview

Treatment information may be distributed across consultation notes, clinical findings, imaging results, treatment plans, and follow-up records.

When these elements are disconnected, clinicians may need to move between multiple screens to understand:

- The patient’s current condition
- The intended course of treatment
- Previous treatment progress
- The next recommended action
- Outstanding follow-up requirements

This project explores a more connected experience across three related areas:

1. Dental consultation workspace
2. Treatment planning
3. Treatment progress tracking

## The Challenge

The main challenge was defining an experience that could:

- Keep clinical context visible during treatment planning
- Support manual and AI-assisted workflows
- Preserve clinician review and decision-making
- Distinguish intended treatment from completed clinical documentation
- Track multiple ongoing treatment journeys independently
- Maintain continuity and traceability across visits

---

## Proposed Experience

### 1. Dental Consultation Workspace

The consultation workspace brings together relevant clinical information within one connected experience.

It allows clinicians to review consultation notes, dental findings, imaging references, tooth-level documentation, and treatment options without repeatedly switching between separate areas of the system.

### 2. Treatment Planning

The treatment planning experience supports both manual and AI-assisted workflows.

AI-generated suggestions are presented as editable recommendations rather than final clinical decisions. Clinicians can review, modify, remove, or add treatments before confirming the plan.

This maintains clinical ownership while allowing technology to support a more efficient planning process.

### 3. Treatment Tracking

The treatment tracker provides a patient-level view of active treatment journeys, including:

- Current treatment status
- Completed progress
- Upcoming actions
- Follow-up requirements
- Monitoring or review needs

Each treatment journey remains independent, helping clinicians monitor parallel treatments without combining unrelated plans or clinical histories.

## Key Product Decisions

### Clinical context stays close to the decision

Relevant findings and supporting information should remain visible while clinicians create or review a treatment plan.

### Clinicians remain the final decision-makers

Manual planning is always available. AI assistance is optional, transparent, and editable.

### Planning, tracking, and documentation serve different purposes

- **Treatment Plan:** Defines the intended course of care
- **Treatment Tracker:** Monitors progress and upcoming actions
- **Case Note:** Documents what occurred during an actual encounter

Maintaining these boundaries improves clarity and traceability.

### Treatment journeys remain independent

A patient may have multiple ongoing treatments. Each journey should retain its own progress, status, and follow-up history.

### Clinical history should not be silently overwritten

Important decisions and progress updates should remain traceable as care evolves.

## What I Learned

This project strengthened my understanding of defining products for complex healthcare workflows.

A useful healthcare product must do more than organize information. It must preserve clinical context, clarify responsibility, support continuity across visits, and account for how different clinical records evolve over time.

The project also reinforced the importance of defining clear boundaries between product features before translating them into product requirements and interactions.
